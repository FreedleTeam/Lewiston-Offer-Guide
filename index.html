Summary

The comparable sales support a purchase between $925,000 and $955,000, with about $940,000 as the most likely number. This house sold for $960,000 in June, in a stronger market, and since then the homes for sale around it have been sitting for months.

Because your offer would depend on selling your home first, and it isn’t quite market ready yet, I’d plan on giving the sellers $10,000 to $20,000 more than we’d otherwise pay. That’s what it costs to ask them to wait on us in a slow time of year. With that, a realistic contingent purchase lands between $935,000 and $975,000. If you end up paying full price, you’re right on target. It’s a great house in a strong neighborhood, and you’d be buying it at a rough moment in the year.

Market context

I used about 13 months of sales, from September 2025 to October 2026, all within about a mile. This house isn’t rare, so there was no need to reach back further, and it means I’m not stretching old prices forward with an appreciation estimate.

In my experience, February through April is the hottest stretch of the year here, with February the peak. This data only has one sale that went under contract in February, but it shows the same pattern. Sales that went under contract March through May closed at a median 99.3% of original list. Sales that went under contract June through September closed at 95.8%. Fall 2025 looked a lot like right now, at 96.4%.

Where the range sits

How I chose comparables

I started with every sale within about a mile and weighted Piney Creek sales most heavily, since they share the neighborhood, schools, and HOA. Lewiston’s dues are about the same as the rest of Piney Creek, so dues didn’t factor in. Sales outside Piney Creek still count, but less, and most of them feed Grandview High instead of Smoky Hill.

I compared homes by price per above-grade square foot, since basement space sells for much less than main and upper floors. Lewiston was built in 1992, and almost every usable comp here was built before 2000, so age differences are small. Square footage comes from public records, except Laredo Street (appraiser) and Quintero Circle (not listed).

In my experience, this price range is holding up a lot better right now than lower price ranges. That’s still true if you’d like to raise the price a little and have the sellers cover some of your closing costs. If you’re financing, the house has to appraise at whatever price we write, including any amount added for closing costs.

Other considerations

Inspection focus

The MLS doesn’t list system ages, so I’ll get them from the seller’s disclosure. On a 1992 house, anything original would be 34 years old. I’d have the inspector look closely at the cement shake roof, the furnace and AC, both water heaters, the fountains and sprinklers, the deck and spiral stairs, the heated bathroom floors, and drainage around the walkout.

Appraisal

If you’re financing, the June sale at $960,000 is the appraiser’s most obvious comp. A price in our range sits at or below that, which helps.

Seller position

They’ve owned it four months. At full price they’d still lose money after selling costs, so I expect them to be realistic. I don’t know their reason for selling yet, and I’m working on that.

Resale

In my view, Upper Knolls, a cul-de-sac, and Smoky Hill schools give it solid resale footing. The finishes that drew you in will narrow the buyer pool a little when it’s your turn to sell, and that’s worth knowing going in.

What this can and can’t tell you

This is the best read I can give you with the data available. It covers about 13 months of sales, not two years. The condition, yard, layout, and location adjustments are my judgment from walking these homes, not measured numbers. No closed sale reported concessions, so I can’t confirm net prices beyond what’s listed. And you toured only one of the comps, Laredo Street.

The numbers tell you what’s supported. What this house is worth to you, given how much you liked it, matters more than any of it.
